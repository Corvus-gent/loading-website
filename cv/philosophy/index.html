Francisco Van Langenhove

Computer scientist, entrepreneur and aspiring philosopher. I want to study philosophy at KU Leuven.

About me

I live in Affligem, am Belgian and speak seven languages. I spent five years studying computer science at Ghent University and came close to finishing my master’s. These days, my work often starts with an idea I want to try out quickly. What I do best is use Codex and Claude to turn that idea into a working prototype, then test and refine it. I program in languages including Python, Rust and C, and have worked with ROS 2.

I founded Corvus, a community of builders that operates entirely in public. I worked on go-to-market for a start-up in Ghent. I recently spent time in San Francisco and New York attending hackathons and speaking with founders.

I used to tutor mathematics, statistics, Python, data science and algorithms. I compete in Brazilian jiu-jitsu (IBJJF).

Why I stopped studying computer science

I was close to finishing my master’s, but the courses and the approach no longer sparked my curiosity as they once did. I did not want to finish the degree simply for the diploma. So I decided to stop.

Why philosophy

I philosophise all day. I work with AI every day, and thinking about mind, language, agency and knowledge has made me a sharper thinker and a better builder.

I have many creative ideas and want to learn how to investigate the questions behind them rigorously, through careful argument and close work with professors. My interests are philosophy of mind, philosophy of AI and technology, the ethics of artificial agents, and epistemology.

I think highly of KU Leuven as a university. For philosophy in particular, I am drawn to the international reputation of the Institute of Philosophy. I also find Leuven a lovely city to spend time in, and it is close enough to Ghent for me to combine my studies with work.
