lost the hackathon / kept the idea

the brief was no brief

So, naturally, I tried to build everything.

There was no assignment. I already have a perfectly unreasonable project called Corvus, so I kept going. A community of builders, built and streamed in public. A place that can keep getting weirder as people contribute.

The plan was a blind AI battle: Muse vs Claude vs Codex. Same prompt, three nests, visitors vote before finding out who built what. Then The Nest Builds Itself: you make a wish, Muse builds the page live. Why stop there? Guess the AI. Prompt Duel. Hack the Raven. Legion tiers as rewards.

These were ideas for the night, not a list of finished features. I had scope creep before I had a working API call. Strong start.

reality entered the chat

I lost.

I lost to 402 Payment Required. I lost to a dashboard/API mismatch: I thought I had $20, while the account endpoint reported $0 in credits. I still do not have a verified explanation for that discrepancy. A key spending limit is not the same thing as money in the account.

Then Codex tried to reserve up to 131,072 output tokens. OpenRouter checked the worst-case cost. My budget did not share my ambition.

There were scripts. There was a local token-cap proxy. There was the script that was supposed to do it all. There was me, getting increasingly fluent in error messages instead of actually building.

Eventually I fell asleep on the floor. At least the floor had no rate limit.

one small thing actually happened

In the very last minute: Ember.

In the OpenRouter playground, Muse Ember built THE NEST BUILDS ITSELF. The readout: 1,755 tokens, $0.001. After an entire evening of plumbing, a model finally made the thing.

What Muse built in the last minute → The linked v0 is a Muse-generated recreation for this recap, not the original saved playground output. The original HTML has not been archived here. It demonstrates the wish-and-raven interaction; it does not secretly call an AI or deploy new pages.

There is also a Muse-built arena preview and Raven Rush. Spark Open generated those pages during the terminal experiments; Codex handled integration and small corrections. Claude and Codex contestants hit 402, so the preview honestly has one contender. Not exactly the battle royale I drew in my head.

the models, without the victory lap

Why I still want to use Muse.

Muse Ember

The small, efficient model for repeated agent work: tools, coding subtasks, routing and extraction. Listed input price: $0.10 per million tokens; output: $0.50 per million.

Muse Ember on OpenRouter ↗

Spark Open + Wonder

Spark Open is the open-weight coding model. Wonder is the frontier reasoning model. Their listings show a 1M-token context window.

OpenRouter’s Ember and Wonder listings describe text, image, video, audio and PDF inputs with text output and a 1M-token context. Model-specific support and prices can change; checked against the linked listings on Nov 9, 2026.
