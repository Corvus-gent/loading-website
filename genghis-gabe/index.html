corvus / field notes · #genghisgabe

genghis gabe.

i'm a 23-year-old dropout from belgium who just flew to san francisco with €40 to build an army. people started calling me genghis gabe. so i went and read about the original. turns out the playbook is 800 years old.

an outcast with nothing

genghis khan was born temüjin, around 1162, on the mongolian steppe. when he was a boy his father was killed and his family was cast out of their clan. no title. no inheritance. no army.

decades later he had united the steppe tribes and started what became the largest contiguous land empire in history.

he didn't do it with a better bloodline. he did it with a better system.

rule 1 · merit over bloodline

the old steppe order ran on aristocratic birth. temüjin broke it. he promoted people for loyalty and results, not for whose son they were. some of his greatest generals started out as common herdsmen, and at least one had once been his enemy.

corvus: nobody gets in for their cv, their followers or their title. you get in for what you build and how you show up. paying moves you up the line. i still pick who gets in.

rule 2 · units of ten

his army was organised in tens. ten soldiers made an arban, ten arbans a hundred, ten hundreds a thousand, ten thousands a tumen. he also deliberately mixed tribes inside those units, so loyalty went to the unit and the mission instead of the old clan.

corvus: the smallest unit is you plus the people you bring. bring three people who finish a daily game and you earn your stripes. different worlds in one room on purpose: founders, rappers, skaters, surfers, fighters.

rule 3 · speed

the empire built a relay network of riders and stations, the yam, that moved messages and orders across a continent in days instead of months. information moved faster than anyone else's.

corvus: we ship daily, in public, with every stat visible. one general, many minds, no meetings about meetings.

different century. same energy.

the part i don't copy

his conquests also killed enormous numbers of people. that's not a footnote and i'm not romanticising it. what i take from him is the organisation: merit, small loyal units, speed. the army i'm building fights for people, not against them.
