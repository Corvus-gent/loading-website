## 1. The night I lost to my own API key (long)

I went to the Meta Muse x OpenRouter hackathon in San Francisco with an extremely reasonable plan:

Build a blind AI arena. Muse vs Claude vs Codex.
Let visitors make a wish and have the Nest build itself.
Add games. Add Legion rewards.
Apparently also solve time management.

There was no assignment, so I kept building Corvus. Then I lost most of the night to 402 Payment Required.

I thought I had $20. The account endpoint showed $0 in credits. I still don't have a verified explanation for that mismatch. A key limit isn't the same thing as an account balance.

Codex was asking for up to 131,072 output tokens. OpenRouter checked the worst-case bill. My budget had other ideas.

So: scripts, a local token-cap proxy, more scripts. I became very good at reading error messages.

Then I fell asleep on the floor. At least the floor had no rate limit.

In the very last minute, Muse Ember built THE NEST BUILDS ITSELF in the OpenRouter playground. My readout: 1,755 tokens. $0.001.

I lost the hackathon. I still want to build the thing.

@alexandr_wang @alexatallah — I really want to build with Muse. Please, credits.

I'll use them for the blind arena, the self-building Nest and games people can actually play. I'll show the work, the costs and the failures in public. Corvus is where I'm building that community.

One tiny Ember made it through. Let's give it a second evening.

## 2. I DON'T PAY — the first counter (long)

I'm starting a series called I DON'T PAY.

The opening line: “I don't pay for my food. Watch.”

The actual plan: walk into a local food spot, ask the owner whether they'd like a short promo video in their language, and agree on a meal in exchange.

I speak seven languages. It's time to put more of them to work outside a language settings menu.

First target: a Turkish bakery or lahmacun place. Börek has a strong argument for being my first business expense.

The owner decides. The meal and deliverable are agreed before I start. No pressure, no secret camera, no recognizable customers without consent. If the answer is no, I pay normally or leave without ordering.

The shop gets a useful standalone clip. I get lunch, feedback and another rep at making something for a real person.

Every episode: the ask, the work, the food, my honest score and whether the deal actually happened.

Episode 1 isn't filmed yet. No imaginary free meals in the trailer.

Students optimize for grades. I optimize for reps.

Which Turkish bakery or lahmacun spot in San Francisco should I ask first? Owners who might enjoy the idea especially welcome.

## 3. Students vs reps (long)

Students optimize for grades. I optimize for reps.

That's the experiment I'm running with Corvus.

A rep is a conversation I actually have.
A video I actually finish.
A pitch someone can actually reject.
A hackathon where I find out what I can ship before the deadline.

Last night, my rep was spending far too long fighting an API key, falling asleep on the floor, and finally getting a tiny Muse Ember page out in the last minute.

Not a clean story. A very informative one.

The next experiment is I DON'T PAY: offer a local shop a useful promo video in its preferred language, agree on a meal in exchange, and show the whole process. The owner can say no. That still counts as a rep at asking respectfully.

I want to keep a public log of hours spent at hackathons, streaming and making videos. The first rule is that the numbers must be real. If I haven't logged an hour, it doesn't magically appear because a dashboard needs to look impressive.

What I'm trying to improve is the gap between an idea and something another human can use.

More reps. Better feedback. A smaller gap.

What's a rep you keep postponing because you're waiting to feel ready?
