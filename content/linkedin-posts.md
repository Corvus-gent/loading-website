## 9. An honest hackathon field note (long)

The night I lost to my own API key.

At the Meta Muse x OpenRouter hackathon in San Francisco, I planned to keep building Corvus: a blind model arena, a self-building Nest and games for a community of builders.

I spent most of the evening getting the connection to work.

402 Payment Required. I thought I had $20, while the account endpoint showed $0 in credits. I haven't verified the reason for that mismatch. Then Codex requested up to 131,072 output tokens and OpenRouter checked whether I could afford the worst case.

That led to scripts and a local token-cap proxy. Eventually, it also led to me asleep on the floor.

In the very last minute, Muse Ember built THE NEST BUILDS ITSELF in the OpenRouter playground. My readout: 1,755 tokens for $0.001.

I lost the hackathon. I did get a very specific lesson: validate one small end-to-end call before designing an entire night around the integration.

Meta / OpenRouter: I really want to build with Muse. Please, credits.

I'd use them to finish the arena, let visitors turn wishes into pages, and build games people can actually use. I'd share the work, costs and failures openly through Corvus.

Thanks to Alexandr Wang and Alex Atallah for the models and the platform. The ambition survived; the scope needs better manners.

## 10. A promo video for lunch (long)

Students optimize for grades. I optimize for reps.

My next Corvus experiment is called I DON'T PAY.

I walk into a local food spot and ask the owner whether they'd like a short promotional video in their language, in exchange for an agreed meal. I speak seven languages; this is a practical way to use them while getting better at asking, filming, editing and delivering.

The first target is a Turkish bakery or lahmacun place in San Francisco. Episode 1 isn't filmed yet.

The agreement comes first. The shop chooses whether the exchange makes sense. We settle the meal, the clip and how it can be used before I start. Customers aren't filmed recognizably without consent. A no is respected: I pay normally or leave without ordering.

Each episode will show the ask, the work, the actual deal and my honest food score. The owner gets a useful standalone promo, not a vague promise of exposure.

I'm also keeping a public hours log for hackathons, streams and video work. No invented baseline. No counting the same hour twice to make a graph look better.

The goal is to shorten the distance between “I could do that” and “I made something useful for someone.”

If you know a Turkish bakery or lahmacun spot in SF whose owner might enjoy this, tell me where to start. Which spot next?
